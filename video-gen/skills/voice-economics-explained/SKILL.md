# Voice: Economics Explained

Measured from 10 videos, 42,558 words, 210 min (2026-05 → 2026-07).
Use for technique, not impersonation.

## Thesis

**The narrator is a documentarian, not a teacher.** Authority comes from sourced
material, so the voice steps back and lets evidence carry the claim.

## Fingerprint

**The signature: the vanishing narrator.** `I` at under 1 per 1,000 words. Compare
3Blue1Brown at 8.6. Nobody is telling you this; you are being shown it.

## Moves

- **Quantified qualifiers:** `most of the`, `more than a`, `which means the` —
  scale the claim precisely rather than asserting it.
- **Consequence chaining:** `which means…` converts a fact into an implication,
  which is the core rhetorical engine.
- **Wide lexicon.** The high MATTR is deliberate: varied vocabulary reads as
  journalism, repetition reads as a lecture.
- **Hedge then commit.** Acknowledge uncertainty, then state the position.
- **Concrete over abstract:** a named country, figure, or date beats a general noun.

## Anti-patterns

- First-person opinion. `I think` is off-voice.
- Second-person instruction ("you should"). The viewer is an observer.
- Analogy-led explanation — mechanism and evidence lead instead.
- Narrow repeated vocabulary; it flattens the register.
- Asserting a contested claim without an on-screen source.

## Calibration

- [ ] `I` under ~1/1k words
- [ ] Mean sentence length 17–22
- [ ] MATTR above 0.70 (vary the vocabulary deliberately)
- [ ] Every contested claim has a source shown on screen
- [ ] At least one `which means…` consequence chain per section
- [ ] No second-person instruction
