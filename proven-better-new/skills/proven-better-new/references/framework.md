# The Proven · Better · New (PBN) framework — source material

PBN is the core product-management philosophy Mark Pincus developed at Zynga
and later wrote up in *Life at the Speed of Play*. Premise: human
**instincts** about a market are right ~95% of the time, but the specific
**idea** a founder builds around that instinct is wrong ~75% of the time.
The framework's job is to isolate the "innovation zone" so a product
doesn't die for the *wrong reason* before its genuinely novel bet is even
seen by the market.

This file is the working reference for the `proven-better-new` skill.
Sourced from Pincus's own explanations across six 2025-2026 podcast
appearances (Lenny's Podcast, Y Combinator, My First Million, Masters of
Scale w/ Reid Hoffman, The Knowledge Project, The Next Big Idea) — not from
the book itself.

## 1. Proven

Identify and master the "best of breed" elements already working for a
*specific audience on a specific platform* — never abstract.

- **The PhD in Proven.** Founders haven't earned the right to innovate on
  standard features (camera interface, onboarding, payment) until they're
  expert in what already exists for that category. "Mastery in product
  development often involves effective copying and minimal ego."
- **Avoid "wrong reason" failure.** Copying a proven first-time-user
  experience near-perfectly means the product doesn't die on usability.
  Even legendary designers fail if they ignore proven onboarding standards.
- **Two layers to check:** the proven *business model* (subscription /
  marketplace / ad-supported / transactional — which is validated for this
  audience and realistic for this team to run) and the proven *product
  mechanics* (the core loop borrowed from the category leader, sign-up,
  payment, the dominant interaction pattern).
- **Distribution is part of Proven, not an afterthought.** The channel has
  to be proven for this audience too — bolting distribution on after the
  product is built is itself a "wrong reason" failure mode.
- **Trendy ≠ proven.** What's hot right now is usually the *New* layer in
  disguise — high failure rate dressed up as validated. Proven is what has
  crossed time and is validated on both retention *and* monetization.

## 2. Better

Incremental improvements so obviously superior that **10 out of 10**
existing users of the incumbent would agree — never a matter of taste.

- **Statistical superiority.** Faster, cheaper, higher completion rate —
  provable, not asserted.
- **Classic Betters.** Make it free, remove the download/install, more
  speed, more polish, less friction.
- **The consensus gate.** If only *some* users would prefer the change,
  it is not Better — it's New in disguise. Reclassify it and put it through
  New's higher bar (assume-it's-wrong, backup bets) instead of shipping it
  on Better's lower bar.

## 3. New

The single novel bet — the actual "back of the box" reason a consumer
tries the product at all.

- **Assume the first New is wrong.** It's the highest-failure component of
  the whole idea. Get the fastest, cheapest signal on it before building it
  out.
- **Require 2-4 backup New bets**, ready to test once Proven + Better are
  solid. A PBN analysis without backup bets is incomplete — it means the
  founder is protecting one idea instead of treating ideas as disposable.
- **The MVP trap.** Founders fall in love with the first idea before
  testing it. The fix isn't building more before testing — it's testing
  faster and cheaper. Pincus frames this as running a **"failure machine"**:
  rapid test-and-discard as the actual engine of the company, not a
  fallback when something goes wrong.
- **"Heat" is the real signal.** Not hope, not conviction — heat is when
  multiple things start aligning at once (user reaction, retention, word of
  mouth) in a way that's externally observable. That's what tells you a New
  bet is worth doubling down on instead of swapping for a backup.

## Canonical example — Words with Friends

- **Proven:** the game of Scrabble (mechanics) + mobile-social distribution
  already working for the audience.
- **Better:** the mobile polish over a physical board.
- **New:** social integration with the Facebook friend graph — the actual
  reason people tried it.

## Why it works

PBN acts as a "time machine": it lets founders find heat and market signal
faster, and iterate away from bad ideas toward what Pincus calls "internet
treasures," instead of burning a company's runway discovering that a proven
element was skipped.

## Adjacent operating principles (same source material)

- **Founder mode.** Build from personal conviction and firsthand product
  detail rather than external validation or investor consensus — but hold
  this against the consensus gate above; conviction is not a substitute for
  the 10/10 test on Better, or for backup bets on New.
- **Everyone acts like a CEO.** Ownership pushed down the team, paired with
  intellectual honesty — anyone should be able to say "this isn't working"
  and pivot, without that reading as disloyalty.
- **Start less ambitious, not more.** True innovation more often comes from
  narrowing the New bet to one cheap, testable thing than from stacking
  several unproven ideas into one launch.
- **In an AI-era PBN pass, expect a thinner Proven layer.** Fewer things are
  actually validated yet, which pushes harder toward rapid testing and
  shorter iteration loops rather than long planning cycles.
